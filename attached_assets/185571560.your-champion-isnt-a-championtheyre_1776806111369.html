Most reps think they’ve got a champion.

They show up to calls.
They say the right things.
They might even drop, “We’re excited about this.”

But here’s the truth:
Most of the time, you’re confusing interest with influence.
And that misread is killing your deals.

🏈 What Is a True Champion?

A true champion isn’t just a fan of your product.
They’re not just helpful.
They’re not just involved.

They’re invested.
They’re influential.
And they’re willing to go to bat for you when you’re not in the room.

Your champion should:

  • Sell internally on your behalf

  • Push for progress across departments

  • Share insights from behind the curtain

  • Get the deal unstuck when it hits friction

If they’re not doing those things, they’re not a champion.
They’re just... nice.

How to Work With a Real Champion

You test them. Early.

Try this:

“If we’re aligned, would you be open to introducing me to your [executive sponsor/finance lead/IT team] so we can keep this on track?”

If they hesitate, ghost, or dodge—you just got clarity.
Don’t get mad. Get strategic.

Once you confirm they’ve got real pull:

  • Loop them into a Mutual Action Plan

  • Arm them with a simple business case they can run with

  • Coach them like they’re your teammate, not just your contact

Build the deal with them—not around them.

The Best Champion? The Economic Buyer.

The person who:

  • Owns the budget

  • Owns the outcome

  • Feels the pain and can fix it

Yes, it’s helpful to have users and influencers on your side.
But your best move is to get above the power line early and stay there.

If your champion can’t sign or at least influence the signature your deal is vulnerable.

🚨 Red Zone Takeaways

  • Pressure-test every champion. Don’t assume—ask.

  • Map your stakeholders. Know who’s who.

  • Elevate early. The longer you wait, the harder it gets.

  • Nice ≠ Power. Your buyer doesn’t need to like you—they need to act.

Deals are won in the Red Zone.
That’s when influence matters most.
And if your champion disappears when it’s time to execute?
They were never a champion—they were a spectator.
